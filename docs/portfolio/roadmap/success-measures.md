# Hiring success measures and review cadence

Baseline: 2026-10-20. These are proposed operating rules, not market conversion benchmarks.

## Outcomes and controllable work

Primary outcome: a suitable role accepted by Garfield. Leading evidence: conversations with relevant employers, interview progression, and offers consistent with confirmed preferences. Application counts help manage throughput; test counts, animation polish, and raw job bookmarks do not establish hiring progress.

At the start, measure actual packet time and opportunity supply before committing to volume. The illustrative capacity model is 15 focused hours/week, initially 1–2 packets then roughly 3–4/week when the loop works. Adjust downward for complex applications and active interviews. Never send a weak application to hit a count.

## Definitions

| Measure | Definition and source |
| --- | --- |
| Qualified opportunities | Distinct employer/requisition records that pass known hard constraints; unknown blocking eligibility is not a pass |
| Packets ready | Job-specific documents and answers have passed QA and are recorded in the tracker; this is not a submission |
| Confirmed applications | Unique applications with authoritative confirmation evidence; an unknown exact submission time does not erase a confirmed send. Retries/duplicate channels do not add counts |
| Employer response | Substantive role-related employer action or reply, including an ATS-delivered interview invitation; acknowledgment/receipt mail is excluded |
| Positive response | Screen/interview invitation or meaningful recruiter interest, excluding rejection and generic acknowledgment |
| Interview progression | Advanced to a later named stage, with date and evidence; store stages separately from interview count |
| Offers and acceptances | Actual written offer and Garfield's recorded acceptance; never infer either from a good interview |
| Eligibility rejection | Rejection explicitly tied to location/work authorization/seniority/other hard constraint, not an inferred reason |
| Preparation effort | Focus time for finding, tailoring, submitting, and interviewing; useful for capacity planning |
| Stale next actions | Nonterminal records whose next-action date passed without update; late response does not automatically mean rejection |

## Cohort reporting

Report counts with their denominator and date range. Suggested views: role lane, source channel, geography/eligibility, and submitted week. Overall rates alone can hide poor matching in one lane.

Positive-response rate = applications in a defined submitted cohort with a positive response / confirmed applications in that cohort. Report how many remain pending and the cohort age. A two-week observation window is a starting review convention, not a universal employer response deadline. Interview-to-next-stage rate uses completed stages with known outcomes, not scheduled interviews. Deduplicate all counts by application ID. If exact submitted_at is unknown, use a supported employer-confirmed date or date range for an explicitly approximate cohort, or keep the application in a date-unknown bucket outside dated rates. Do not substitute the later discovery/capture date as if it were the submission date. Report this excluded bucket so total confirmed submissions still reconcile.

Do not count drafts, submission-unknown records, or automatic acknowledgments as success. Zero applications means rate unavailable, not 0% candidate quality. Zero responses in a small or recent cohort is a signal to inspect, not a statistically sound estimate.

## Working cadence

- Each execution session: inspect due actions and actual interviews, reconcile uncertain submissions, choose one bounded task, and update the record before stopping.
- Two or three sourcing blocks a week: refresh official postings, qualify the best matches, and replenish the queue. These are manual planning blocks; no automation was created.
- Once a week, about 30 minutes: count real outcomes, look at matured cohorts, inspect bottlenecks, choose one adjustment, and set next week's capacity. Save a dated private review.
- Before each interview: use the posting snapshot and submitted packet, prepare role-relevant project and ownership stories, and note questions for the employer. Debrief afterward while details are fresh.

## Decision rules

| Observed pattern | Investigate / change |
| --- | --- |
| Too few qualified roles | Check query breadth, sources, seniority, and real location restrictions; discuss constraints rather than silently relaxing them |
| High eligibility rejection | Improve hard filtering and candidate-answer confirmation before sending more |
| Many ready packets but few confirmed sends | Inspect access, form friction, attachment issues, and submission evidence; reconcile unknown outcomes first |
| Roughly 10 mature, well-matched applications with no positive response | Review targeting, first-page resume evidence, proof links, and credibility with Garfield; change one factor, do not declare the candidate unqualified |
| Screens but little advancement | Review role-specific story, scope/seniority fit, compensation/time-zone answers, and answers to recurring questions |
| Technical rounds expose the same gap | Add targeted practice or one bounded proof improvement; do not redesign the whole portfolio |
| Too much time spent per packet | Reuse verified facts and document components, reduce unnecessary cover letters, and focus on stronger fit |
| Active interviews exceed available time | Reduce new application volume and prioritize preparation and follow-through |

The 10-application trigger is a practical review prompt, not statistical significance or a promised conversion threshold. Record concrete feedback separately from interpretations. Never invent rejection reasons.
