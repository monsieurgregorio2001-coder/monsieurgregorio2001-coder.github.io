Updated: 2026-10-20 (Asia/Manila)

## Core promise

Present Garfield as someone who can understand an ambiguous need, shape a coherent idea, bring people into the decisions, and carry an AI product through to useful delivery. Technical implementation supports that promise. Taste appears in the choices, clarity, restraint, and finish of the work.

The user's thesis is a positioning principle: as AI makes implementation more accessible, the judgment applied to a problem becomes more valuable. Do not turn that into an empirical assertion that all engineers have equal skills or that technical depth no longer matters. Demonstrate the principle through project decisions and human outcomes.

Use one coherent public identity and tailor the angle to the opportunity. Do not require a narrow title selection before starting research. Applied AI Engineer remains a useful professional anchor from the existing materials; it is not the only acceptable target. Titles such as AI Solutions Architect or GTM Engineer are search/fit lanes, not new employment titles to insert into history.

## Working public expression

Headline direction: **I turn complex workflows into clear, useful AI products.**

Supporting direction: **I work across problem discovery, system design, and delivery, connecting what people need with what the technology can support.**

Treat these as writing directions to refine against confirmed experience. Do not publish unverified customer-discovery or ownership claims merely because the sentence sounds good. The current live site and resume have not been rewritten by this planning update. No single sentence is mandatory.

Make the first screen answer: What kinds of problems does he handle? What can I inspect? How does he think? How do I reach him? Keep the title understandable to a recruiter and the work specific enough for an engineer. Let the projects carry personality.

## What the positioning must prove

| Dimension | Observable evidence | Case-study question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Taste | Coherent typography, hierarchy, interaction, language, and deliberate selection; artifacts show purposeful editing | What did you choose, refine, or leave out, and why was that appropriate for this audience? |
| Judgment | Alternatives, constraints, failure modes, tradeoffs, honest limits, and acceptance evidence | How did you decide what good looked like, and what would change your decision? |
| People skills | Real examples of listening, explaining, aligning expectations, handling disagreement, enabling others, or improving handoff | Who needed to understand or support the decision, and what did you do to help? |
| Conceptualization | A clear account of how a messy situation became a useful product idea and a bounded first release | What was the actual problem, how did you reframe it, and why this solution? |
| Systematic thinking | Domain rules, dependencies, ownership boundaries, data flow, validation, and operational recovery | How do the parts work together, and what happens when something fails? |
| Delivery | Inspectable functioning work, reliable access, readable artifacts, reproducible checks, and appropriately sourced results | What can someone verify now, and what remains uncertain? |

These are evidence prompts, not self-awarded personality scores. A good-looking page alone does not establish customer empathy; a workflow diagram alone does not establish enterprise architecture experience. Qualitative outcomes are useful when attributable and specific. Do not invent satisfaction, adoption, revenue, conversion, or leadership metrics.

## Project presentation

- **Living Mindmap / Kuru:** show how human and agent work coexist, why duplication/provenance matter, and how the local system makes those choices legible. Use the released name and a real tool interaction.
- **PaperLab:** show how a difficult concept becomes understandable through computation and interaction. Retain toy-model limits. Explain a real teaching/representation choice instead of asserting communication talent.
- **Online Paluwagan:** show domain understanding, fairness, integer-money rules, exception handling, and human control. Keep its demo, pre-generated reminder, and simulated-delivery boundaries visible.
- **Slop Editorial:** show a coherent editorial concept, the relationship between the critique and the interaction, curation/QA decisions, and operational safeguards. Record actual human choices and iterations; the concept or generated imagery alone does not establish sole design authorship. Its existing About page claims personal image checks and a repository review trail, which this handover has not substantiated; recover or qualify that claim before treating curation as verified proof.
- **Permitted employment stories:** show problem framing, personal contribution, collaboration, constraints, and deployment/results using material that may be disclosed. Confirm the source first and anonymize only with a sound basis.

A case study should contain: situation, audience, personal role, concept, alternatives, people involved, important decision, system/interaction, validation, outcome, and limitation. Omit irrelevant headings and keep the story readable. Attribute team contributions and AI assistance accurately. Evidence can be a diagram, screenshot, code path, recorded walkthrough, dated check, or concrete collaboration account.

## Portfolio and document design brief

Use clear hierarchy, strong editorial selection, intentional spacing, legible type, coherent interaction, and accurate visuals. Make the strongest work easy to find; give each project a reason to exist in the selection. Show a small amount of thinking with the work: one clear choice and its consequence is more useful than a long list of tools.

The existing editorial system and Five Rooms prototype are sources to evaluate, not decisions settled by this positioning. Adopt a design slice only when it serves the audience and can be finished and checked. Motion should clarify continuity and respect reduced motion. Keep normal navigation, readable fallbacks, and phone use reliable.

Resume and application documents should feel edited and specific. Keep a readable, parsable document and use role-relevant supported examples. For architecture roles, foreground decisions and communication. For GTM roles, foreground the actual business workflow and integration outcomes. For builder roles, foreground concept-to-delivery choices. Preserve one verified experience history across all variants.

## First useful positioning slice

Start with one concrete project decision and one real collaboration story. A compact decision receipt is enough: what needed deciding, the credible alternatives, why this choice suited the constraint, and what evidence would change the decision.

Living Mindmap is a useful systems/agent-workflow anchor; PaperLab provides a clear conceptualization/explanation angle. Slop can carry editorial judgment when the individual decisions and provenance are substantiated. Select the pair best suited to the actual role instead of writing a new case study for every title. Keep a private story bank using real examples of ambiguity, disagreement, explanation, prioritization, collaboration, and a correction. The template lives at workspace _career/templates/story.md; no completed story is implied.
