# ADR-0004: Broad AI roles, with taste and judgment as the throughline

Date: 2026-10-20 (Asia/Manila)
State: accepted
Decided by: Garfield for the role openness and narrative principle; the agent supplies the operational framing and draft copy.
Authority: Reply to the roadmap-target question. Garfield named AI Engineer, vibe coding, AI Solution Architect, GTM Engineer, and related work. He asked for taste, judgment, people skills, conceptualization, and systematic thinking to be the public-facing principle without repeating his words literally.
Related: [Positioning brief](../roadmap/positioning.md), [SPEC-0004](../specs/SPEC-0004-hiring-roadmap.md)
Supersedes: The assumption that role-family direction was entirely unknown or limited to the earlier two-lane search. Does not change unknown practical constraints or validate experience claims.

## Decision

Keep the opportunity search open across AI engineering, AI product building/prototyping, solutions architecture/engineering, GTM engineering, and related roles. Evaluate actual responsibilities, seniority, eligibility, and evidence separately.

Use one coherent public identity whose work demonstrates considered choices, useful concepts, communication, and system design. Adapt the application emphasis by role; retain the same truthful employment history and ownership. Treat taste as a visible quality of the decisions and the finish, rather than a claim of superiority or a decorative layer.

## Rationale and consequences

A narrow tool/title pitch would miss the requested breadth; a long headline of unrelated job titles would obscure the person and the work. The chosen approach makes problem framing and judgment legible while keeping applications specific.

The positioning brief supplies concrete proof prompts, a role/evidence matrix, and design/copy guidance. It does not approve a particular visual redesign, confer architect or GTM experience, establish business outcomes, or authorize new factual claims. Countries, arrangement, compensation, availability, seniority, and time budget remain unconfirmed unless separately answered.

The underlying AI/taste thesis is a user-selected narrative principle, not a research finding that technical skill is equal across people.
