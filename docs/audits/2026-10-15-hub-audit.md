# Hub audit — 2026-10-15

Scope: the portfolio hub at commit `f3343f5` ("Refresh Slop Editorial card"), rendered locally at 1280×800 and 390×844 in light and dark. The live URL was not reachable from the audit environment, so live-only checks (GitHub Pages headers, real CDN timing, project subpaths) are noted as unverified.

## Verdict

Technically sound, visually safe. The hub does the editorial-minimal thing competently but stops short of distinctive. The copy is strong and the case-card structure (Proves / Problem / Build / Result) is the best idea on the page. What's missing is depth: a hero that feels alive, project pages that let a reviewer go deeper than a card, and a few considered moments of motion and craft that signal "this person has taste" without shouting.

## Strengths

1. **Copy.** "I turn messy operations into working AI systems" is a real positioning line. Every card has a measured result with real numbers (tests, ms, KB, Lighthouse scores). This is rare and should be protected.
2. **Card structure.** Proves → Problem → Build → Result is a reviewer-friendly rhythm. Keep it.
3. **Engineering hygiene.** No build step, no tracking, self-hosted fonts, tabular numerals on figures, skip link, `role=list` resets, lazy images with explicit dimensions, dark theme through tokens.
4. **Proof points** in the hero (8 devs / 4 products, 12 MCP tools, magna cum laude) are concrete.

## Weaknesses

### Design

1. **Hero is static and generic.** Large serif headline on a flat background is the default "tasteful portfolio" look of 2024–26. Nothing moves, nothing is specific to *AI systems*. This is where the ripple glyph field belongs: an ambient, low-contrast field of characters that responds to the cursor and ripples on click reads as "built with language models" without saying it.
2. **All four cards have identical weight.** Four equal cards in a column means nothing is the flagship. Living Mindmap (MCP) is the strongest fit for the target roles (agents, MCP) but sits third.
3. **Screenshots are full-page crops at tiny scale.** The 1200×750 shots shrink to ~500 px wide in the card; UI text becomes illegible and the images read as grey rectangles. Cropped detail shots or device-framed hero crops would show the work better.
4. **No depth beyond the card.** There is nowhere to go after reading a card except the live demo or the raw repo. A reviewer who wants "how did you build this and what did you decide" has to read source. Case-study pages (one per project) are the standard expectation for this level.
5. **Surface hierarchy is flat.** Cards use `--surface` on `--paper` with a 1 px rule and almost no shadow on hover (`0 1px 2px / .04`). It is correct but inert. Hover and focus states on cards and buttons barely register.
6. **No visual identity beyond type.** No mark, no colour accent beyond one blue, no texture, no illustration system. The favicon is a bare SVG. The wordmark is just the name in Fraunces.
7. **About section is text-only.** No photo, no timeline, no sense of the person. For a role where the customer-facing part matters (FDE, Applied AI Architect), a face and a short arc helps.
8. **Tags are decorative.** Pills like "React", "TypeScript" are not linked, not filterable, and carry no weight. Either make them do something or trim them.

### Content

9. **Four "proves" lines point in four directions** (theory, fintech, MCP, taste). That is honest but reads scattered for a hub that should sell one thing. A short framing line, or ordering by relevance to the target role, would fix it.
10. **No resume link.** Nothing on the page offers a PDF. Recruiters look for it.
11. **No "now" or "writing" surface.** A single line about what you are currently building, or one or two write-ups, is what separates "built some projects" from "practitioner".
12. **Proof points are fine but unlinked.** "12 MCP tools" should scroll to the Mindmap card.

### Engineering / repo

13. **No docs directory.** Decisions (why no framework, why these fonts, why `projects.json`) exist only in commit messages. No changelog, no audit trail, no tracker.
14. **Scripts are Mac-only.** `capture.mjs`, `shoot.mjs`, `og.mjs` hardcode `/Applications/Google Chrome.app`. Fine for one person, brittle for CI.
15. **No CI.** No link check, no HTML validation, no Lighthouse, no axe run on push. All of these are cheap for a static site.
16. **`projects.json` is fetched client-side.** Means a flash of "Loading projects." and no cards when JS fails (noscript is a plain list, not cards). For four items, inlining the data or rendering the cards at build time is simpler and faster.
17. **No `CLAUDE.md` / contributor guide.** The README explains structure but not conventions (copy voice, where numbers come from, what counts as a "result").
